why the Support DB copy of Cellarium exists, and when a nightly backup is good enough versus when it is not?

Why a separate support DB copy exists
1. Isolation from the production load = debugging queries can be expensive (select *s). Running them against production risks lock contention, buffer pool churn.
2. Access control - easy to grant engineer staff read access to a copy than to production, where creds are scoped
3. Safety - a copy makes it impossible for a support investigation to accidentally mutate production data
4. Point-in-time consistency for investigation - a static snapshot lets you investigae "what did the data look like at time t" without changes happening under you mid-query

When nightly backup is good enough
A: The support need is investigative - what did this record look like yesterday - reproducing a bug
The workload is read-heavy and exploratory rather than time-critical

When nightly backup is not good enough
A: During a live fix, Active incident triage, a 12-23-hour-old snapshot can be misleading, Data that changes frequently and spport needs it now. and anything involving records created or modified after the last nightly run

Rule of thumb: nightly is fine for what happened and not fine for whats happening or did my change take effect
